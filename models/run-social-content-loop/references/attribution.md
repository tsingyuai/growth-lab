# Social content attribution

Use this reference before reviewing outcomes or connecting platform metrics to product results.

## Measurement levels

Separate each level:

1. Platform exposure: impressions, reach, reads, views, followers reached.
2. Platform engagement: likes, comments, saves, shares, follows, profile visits.
3. Traffic movement: link clicks, landing page visits, QR scans, referral codes, bio-link visits.
4. Product conversion: registration, email capture, workspace creation, trial start, purchase.
5. Activation: first meaningful Product value, as defined by the relevant activation Model or product Memory.
6. Guardrails: negative comments, unfollows, reports, account warnings, support burden, privacy issues, trust complaints.

## Attribution requirements

Use the strongest available link:

- platform post URL or article ID;
- UTM parameters or campaign codes;
- short link analytics;
- dedicated landing page;
- referral code, coupon, or invite code;
- QR code with campaign parameter;
- platform export and product event window aligned by timestamp;
- experiment or staged rollout design.

When no direct link exists, state association only. Do not claim the content caused registration or activation from timing alone.

## Comparison safety

Compare only compatible windows, platforms, audience scopes, account states, and content types. Do not compare a boosted post with an unboosted organic post without labeling the difference.

For Xiaohongshu, saves and comments can indicate usefulness or resonance but are not product activation. For WeChat, reads and shares can indicate distribution but not registration without a link or code path.

## Review decision

Choose one next action:

- continue collecting;
- create another variant;
- adapt to another platform;
- revise hook, asset, CTA, or landing path;
- improve measurement;
- pause the topic;
- stop the campaign;
- request publishing or analytics authorization.

Tie the decision to the predeclared learning goal, platform metrics, product metrics, guardrails, and evidence quality.
